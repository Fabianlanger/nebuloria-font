Display typeface specimen. Solid black letterforms on a plain warm off-white background. Flat vector look: no colour, no texture, no 3D, no shadow, no metallic shine.
Style: a blend of Victorian cast-iron conservatory ironwork and Y2K tech lettering. High contrast, as if drawn with a broad-nib pen held at 45 degrees.
Rules for every letter:
1. Stroke pairs: every letter part is a heavy main stroke plus a thin wavy hairline "tendril" (like a wire) that starts at the same point and then diverges.
2. Mirrored weight profiles: the main stroke stays at full weight for most of its length and tapers to a very thin hairline at its free end. The tendril is a hairline all along and swells slightly into a small teardrop at its free end.
3. Subtle negative space: at most one small leaf-shaped cut-out per stroke pair, only where the tendril touches the main stroke.
4. Tech geometry: outer corners are chamfered at 45 degrees. Diagonals run only at 45 degrees, from north-east to south-west. A curve first runs straight, then turns through a short 45-degree segment, then curls.
5. Few leaves: at most one leaf terminal per letter, only at its top-most free end. In letters with a bowl (a, b), one small leaf grows from the inside of the bowl into the centre of the counter.
6. Stems stand on an asymmetric serif foot, longer to the right.
Avoid: green or any colour, fairy-tale or storybook vine lettering, leaves stuck on top of strokes, flowers, art nouveau excess, art deco, gold.
